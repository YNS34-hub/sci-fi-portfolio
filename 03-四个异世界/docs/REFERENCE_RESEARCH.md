# Reference Research

## Method and Identity Resolution

Research was completed from official sites, current captured imagery, creator credits and independent award or gallery records. The references are treated as principle sources only. Their brands, code, copy, media, layouts, models and recognizable animation sequences will not be reused.

The names in the brief contain three important ambiguities:

- `Twice` is the Paris and Oslo film duo at [twicetwice.tv](https://www.twicetwice.tv/), not the unrelated Paris graphic-design practice at twice-studio.com.
- `Joseph` is Ecuadorian multidisciplinary developer Joseph Santamaria at [joseph-san.com](https://joseph-san.com/).
- `Adrien Lay` is a misspelling. The source portfolio list links to creative developer Adrien Lamy at [adrienlamy.fr](https://adrienlamy.fr/). The current site, Landing Love record and the list entry all support this correction.

## 1. Alche, Inc.

1. **Official address:** [https://alche.studio/](https://alche.studio/)
2. **Owner:** Alche, Inc., a Japanese immersive-entertainment studio working with Unreal Engine, Fortnite Creative and large-scale digital worlds.
3. **Visual keywords:** technical shrine, refractive emblem, black grid chamber, programmable material, game-engine precision, controlled ultraviolet.
4. **First viewport:** The composition is organized around one huge translucent triangular `A` suspended in a curved grid room. The wordmark sits behind it at architectural scale. Tiny navigation, live-looking material parameters and a compact news feed occupy the perimeter, leaving the emblem as the singular focus.
5. **Typography:** An oversized geometric grotesk wordmark is paired with small monospaced interface copy. The contrast between monumental brand type and instrument-panel annotations creates authority.
6. **Color:** Near-black and graphite carry most of the surface. White type and a narrow range of cool violet, icy cyan and reflective highlights are emitted by the 3D object rather than spread as decorative gradients.
7. **Image or video:** Portfolio media becomes environmental content and surfaces inside the continuous visual system. Static corporate photography is not the opening device.
8. **3D and WebGL:** Three.js, GLSL and a custom rendering layer power multiple synchronized scenes. The official license page also identifies GSAP, Lenis, Swup, post-processing and Blender source files. The hero object exposes material and quaternion language so the technology reads as studio identity.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** WebGL state, page state and navigation transitions are coordinated. The 3D world persists while page content changes, avoiding the feeling of unrelated HTML sections.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Pointer movement affects orientation and perception of the central object. The technical controls imply direct manipulation without turning the hero into a generic orbit-controls demo.
11. **Most memorable element:** A single brand glyph behaves like an artifact inside a calibrated virtual testing chamber.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Give advanced technology one legible symbol; let micro-interface data amplify material credibility; preserve one spatial world across transitions.
13. **Copy risks:** The triangular `A`, curved grid chamber, exposed roughness/quaternion labels, ultraviolet material and exact full-screen composition are highly distinctive and must not be repeated together.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** Heavy first-load cost, visually tiny annotations at 4K and the possibility that interface decoration competes with essential navigation.
15. **Original transformation:** Use a different object grammar, lighting logic and story. For the luxury route, a tensioned mechanical reliquary can react to cursor pressure and scroll-driven assembly without grids, purple light or editable material readouts.

## 2. bychudy.

1. **Official address:** [https://www.bychudy.com/](https://www.bychudy.com/)
2. **Owner:** Portfolio of Miłosz Chudy, art director, graphic designer, photographer and stylist. The Framer project credit identifies Asia Gawron as the site creator.
3. **Visual keywords:** messy desktop, personal archive, anti-grid, macOS memory, visual culture, controlled clutter, intimate self-portrait.
4. **First viewport:** A blurred self-portrait fills a pale desktop. Dozens of project icons are scattered around the face while a macOS-like dock anchors the bottom. The body of work is visible before any conventional introduction.
5. **Typography:** System-like sans typography keeps labels familiar and small. Project titles remain terse. The apparent neutrality lets the varied artwork become the color and texture system.
6. **Color:** A cool off-white operating-system ground and translucent dock are punctured by many saturated project thumbnails. Color arrives through work, not a global accent.
7. **Image or video:** Each icon is an authored cover. Opening a project reveals descriptions, stills and clips inside window-like overlays.
8. **3D and WebGL:** The identity is primarily DOM and Framer interaction rather than cinematic WebGL. Depth comes from windows, blur, stacking and spatial distribution.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** The homepage behaves more like a bounded desktop than a long page. The project window becomes the narrative container, reducing page changes.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Visitors click scattered icons, open macOS-style pop-ups and can drag selected interface elements. Exploration is non-linear but anchored by familiar desktop behavior.
11. **Most memorable element:** A portfolio converted into a personal computer desktop where the artist's face is literally behind the work.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Use a familiar operating model to make unconventional navigation understandable; let content quantity become composition; make personal identity part of the interface rather than a biography section.
13. **Copy risks:** A Mac dock, desktop icons, draggable windows, self-portrait wallpaper and scattered work layout as one combined system would be imitation.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** Small icon labels, hidden project hierarchy, overlap chaos and a weaker mobile experience if the desktop metaphor is preserved too literally.
15. **Original transformation:** Borrow the principle of operable content, not the operating system. The experimental route will use a physical stage with typed objects, gravity wells and a consequence-based toy, without desktop chrome or computer nostalgia.

## 3. Twice.

1. **Official address:** [https://www.twicetwice.tv/](https://www.twicetwice.tv/)
2. **Owner:** Two brothers based in Paris and Oslo producing commercials, stories and personal films.
3. **Visual keywords:** director's reel, cinematic restraint, image-first, human movement, nocturnal color, chaptered filmography.
4. **First viewport:** A high-quality widescreen film still or moving image owns almost the whole screen. Branding and navigation are reduced so the visitor enters through a cinematic moment, not an agency pitch.
5. **Typography:** A precise contemporary sans and compact lowercase wordmark provide a quiet frame. Type rarely challenges the footage for attention.
6. **Color:** The interface stays largely black, white and transparent. Each film controls the temporary palette, including deep cyan shadows, sodium warmth and decisive costume colors.
7. **Image or video:** Full-bleed moving image is the primary language. Projects are grouped as stories, commercials and personal work, creating a legible editorial taxonomy.
8. **3D and WebGL:** The memorable quality is not 3D spectacle. It comes from video scale, sequencing, crop, loading and transitions that respect the source frame.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** Project changes behave like cuts or reel advances. The pace is deliberate and content-led rather than driven by constant interface effects.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Hover and cursor position expose titles or advance project browsing while leaving the film surface intact.
11. **Most memorable element:** The confidence to let one excellent film frame carry the entire first impression.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Make authored media the interface; use restraint to increase perceived production value; build rhythm through edit decisions rather than many simultaneous effects.
13. **Copy risks:** Reusing the same full-screen director-reel taxonomy, quiet lower-case wordmark and exact film-to-film transition would be too close.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** Weak footage would collapse the design; text discoverability can suffer; video bandwidth and autoplay policies need graceful handling.
15. **Original transformation:** Site 03 will borrow editorial confidence but become a photographic culture journal, using image crop, horizontal chapters and variable type as the protagonists rather than a director reel.

## 4. Joseph.

1. **Official address:** [https://joseph-san.com/](https://joseph-san.com/)
2. **Owner:** Joseph Santamaria, Ecuadorian multidisciplinary developer and interactive software designer.
3. **Visual keywords:** submerged architecture, teal void, spiritual dimension, procedural atmosphere, technical authorship, scroll descent.
4. **First viewport:** A dark, teal spatial environment sits behind a large two-line proposition. The 3D scene remains legible as architecture rather than decorative stars. Sound, contact, frame-rate and authorship notes sit at the edges.
5. **Typography:** A thin, wide sans with selective white emphasis overlays the world. Monospaced and bracketed utility text reinforces the software-engineering register.
6. **Color:** Deep blue-green, soot and dim white dominate. Small warm particles give scale without turning the scene into a neon space background.
7. **Image or video:** Project media appears within a larger 3D journey and remains tied to the creator's technical narrative.
8. **3D and WebGL:** Custom Three.js scenes, environments and post-processing lead the experience. GSAP scroll orchestration reveals new spatial arrangements rather than keeping one passive background.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** The page describes itself as unfolding. Scrolling moves through layered spaces and proof points, so progression feels like entering deeper dimensions.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Pointer movement affects view and atmosphere, while sound is an explicit optional layer. The interface also exposes contact and works without abandoning the immersive context.
11. **Most memorable element:** A professional portfolio framed as a personal cosmology, with authorship and worldview embedded into the 3D narrative.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Connect spatial art direction to personal meaning; combine proof of capability with atmosphere; keep edge UI sparse enough to preserve the world.
13. **Copy risks:** Teal submerged buildings, warm drifting motes, thin white upper-left headline and bottom-edge technical credits together form a recognizable composition.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** Low contrast, tiny peripheral copy, expensive rendering and conventional "scroll to explore" instructions.
15. **Original transformation:** The unknown-world route will use a bright mineral signal, evolving spatial phases and a controllable polyphonic artifact. It will avoid underwater architecture, creator biography and teal darkness.

## 5. Cappen.

1. **Official address:** [https://cappen.com/](https://cappen.com/)
2. **Owner:** Cappen, an interactive digital studio working from Miami and São Paulo.
3. **Visual keywords:** typographic machine, black and white, editorial scale, modular motion, embedded sculpture, confident studio system.
4. **First viewport:** Huge black grotesk type builds the statement `BUILDING TOMORROW FOR TODAY` across the white canvas. A small dark 3D image interrupts the type. A compact studio statement occupies the top right, producing deliberate tension between extreme and tiny scales.
5. **Typography:** Heavy custom-looking display sans leads. Smaller neutral sans and occasional technical copy provide contrast. Letterform alternates in the wordmark make the system recognizable without adding many colors.
6. **Color:** Strict black and white create the core identity, with project media and selective warm yellow adding controlled variation later.
7. **Image or video:** Case-study media appears in large, scroll-driven modules. The opening uses one sculptural image as punctuation rather than a full hero photograph.
8. **3D and WebGL:** 3D objects and fluid simulation appear as signature moments within an otherwise typography-led system. Technology is inserted where it can break the grid.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** Layered typography, pinned modules, project sequences and fluid transitions turn a studio index into ambient movement.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Hover and cursor input reveal project detail, influence fluid or image effects and strengthen the feeling that the grid is responsive.
11. **Most memorable element:** Oversized text behaves like architecture while a small dark object cuts a physical aperture through it.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Use extreme scale contrast; let one small material element disrupt a typographic field; repeat a modular grammar while changing section behavior.
13. **Copy risks:** The exact three-line slogan composition, strict black-on-white treatment, tiny top-right paragraph and central black object should not be reproduced.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** Some tiny explanatory text, a long agency page and repeated brand claims can reduce clarity. The contact bot risks feeling detached if it is only a novelty.
15. **Original transformation:** Site 01 may use scale contrast but will place type around a mechanical ritual, not inside a white editorial studio grid. Site 03 may use large type but with image-driven color and different scroll logic.

## 6. The Studio of Sergey Lisovskiy

1. **Official address:** [https://lisovskiy.work/](https://lisovskiy.work/)
2. **Owner:** Sergey Lisovskiy, also presented as Siarhiej Lisoŭski, working across identity, web design, development, campaigns and motion.
3. **Visual keywords:** office artifact, giant floppy disk, utilitarian grotesk, graphic wit, intentionally awkward scale, archival computing.
4. **First viewport:** The studio name sits in a large clean block at the upper left. A giant illustrated floppy disk occupies the center. A vertical list of fields crowds the right edge, making a playful triangle between identity, object and capability.
5. **Typography:** Neutral neo-grotesk type is intentionally plain. The floppy label introduces bitmap and monospace associations. Scale and collision supply character instead of decorative font mixing.
6. **Color:** White, black and warm gray dominate, with a narrow yellow-orange bar on the floppy label.
7. **Image or video:** Project images emerge through an index-led system. The archive is broad, but the presentation remains authored rather than becoming a standard card grid.
8. **3D and WebGL:** The opening's impact is graphic and object-based, not dependent on photoreal 3D. Illustration, transforms and motion provide dimensional presence.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** Long capability and project indexes become moving typographic fields. Transitions preserve a raw, system-like directness.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Hover states and object reactions turn lists into previews and give the intentionally simple surface energy.
11. **Most memorable element:** A huge floppy disk labeled as the container for the studio's best ideas.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Choose one culturally specific object; create wit through scale; let a simple palette support dense information.
13. **Copy risks:** Giant floppy disk, `MY BEST IDEAS` label, upper-left studio title and right-side stacked discipline list are a complete signature and cannot be reused.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** The density can appear chaotic; the artifact metaphor may overshadow the work; intentionally awkward overlap still needs accessibility.
15. **Original transformation:** The gallery can learn from singular object navigation, but it will use four moving thresholds rather than retro computer artifacts. Site 02 will use real physics, not office nostalgia.

## 7. Stiff.

1. **Official address:** [https://stiff.madebybuzzworthy.com/](https://stiff.madebybuzzworthy.com/)
2. **Owner:** Working Stiff Films, a Baltimore comedy production studio. Site concept, design and development by Buzzworthy Studio.
3. **Visual keywords:** comedy poster, retro advertising, cream and red, cartoon mascots, cinematic timing, loud typography, handcrafted mischief.
4. **First viewport:** Chunky cream and red type stages `WE MAKE LAUGHS THAT LOOK DAMN GOOD` against charcoal. A hand-drawn wind-up-teeth character interrupts the word, while dimensional blobs and film imagery build a lively surrounding world.
5. **Typography:** Heavy condensed and block display faces deliver punch lines. A restrained uppercase navigation font keeps actions readable. Type changes scale like comedy timing.
6. **Color:** Charcoal, aged cream and saturated red build a vintage poster system. Yellow and soft 3D whites appear in characters and accents.
7. **Image or video:** Commercial reels stay central. Video is integrated with character illustration and graphic transitions rather than placed into generic thumbnails.
8. **3D and WebGL:** Communication Arts identifies Three.js and WebGL for characters and horizontal storytelling, with GSAP and custom JavaScript for motion. 3D supports the comedic brand rather than demonstrating technical futurism.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** Horizontal storytelling, character-driven scenes and cinematic cuts use timing as a narrative tool. Headers enter like punch lines; a van travels through the story.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Hover and scroll trigger expressions, movement and playful micro-reactions. Every response is in character.
11. **Most memorable element:** A site about comedy actually performs comedy through scroll timing and living mascots.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Let brand voice govern motion physics; turn transitions into narrative cuts; make custom characters carry repeated interaction feedback.
13. **Copy risks:** Red-cream-charcoal retro comedy palette, wind-up teeth, rolling van, exact slogan and horizontal biography sequence must remain unique to Stiff.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** High video count creates performance pressure; constant jokes can compete with work; heavy display type can lose clarity at smaller sizes.
15. **Original transformation:** Site 02 can inherit consequence-based humor and physical feedback while using abstract typographic creatures, a different primary palette and a user-authored interaction rather than mascots or retro advertising.

## 8. Thingy & Thingy.

1. **Official address:** [https://www.thingyandthingy.com/](https://www.thingyandthingy.com/)
2. **Owner:** Thingy & Thingy, an anti-advertising advertising agency. Creative direction by Baillat Studio, web design by Simon Langlois, development and animation by Samuel Charpentier, motion by Luce Ruissy, brand and illustration by Studio Bingo.
3. **Visual keywords:** anti-serious, fluorescent collage, pizza logic, horoscope, comic speech, absurd utility, cultural prank.
4. **First viewport:** A loud, colorful branded world states the anti-advertising position quickly. Type, illustration and image cutouts behave as equal actors rather than settling into a clean agency hero.
5. **Typography:** Large sans display type collides with sticker-like labels, comic phrasing and intentionally unexpected type accents. Hierarchy is maintained through scale and strong color fields.
6. **Color:** Coral, hot pink, acid green, sky blue and off-white are used as committed areas, not subtle accents.
7. **Image or video:** Pizzas, cats, dinosaurs, branded work and surreal cutouts are treated as authored cultural objects. The visuals are specific enough to make the agency's attitude instantly legible.
8. **3D and WebGL:** The primary strength is layered illustration, compositing and motion rather than one photoreal 3D hero.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** Sections change composition frequently. Movement behaves like animated advertising ephemera and keeps the long manifesto from becoming a static wall.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Buttons such as `Question Everything` and the horoscope compatibility checker invite participation. The checker converts name and star sign into a deliberately over-serious cosmic result, turning lead generation into a joke with a useful ending.
11. **Most memorable element:** A complete interactive compatibility mechanism that expresses the agency voice and ends by steering the user toward contact.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Make the conversion mechanism itself branded; sustain wildness with clear reading order; use absurdity to expose a real positioning idea.
13. **Copy risks:** Pizza imagery, horoscope copy, cosmic compatibility, the anti-advertising phrase and fluorescent collage should never be repeated as a bundle.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** Long ironic copy, sensory overload, inaccessible color collisions and interaction that becomes funny only once.
15. **Original transformation:** Site 02 will include a machine where visitors feed words into a typographic gravity field and receive an authored visual specimen. It will create a useful collectible result without horoscope, pizza or agency satire.

## 9. Adrien Lamy

1. **Official address:** [https://adrienlamy.fr/](https://adrienlamy.fr/)
2. **Owner:** Adrien Lamy, independent creative developer based in Paris with a long-running 3D and shader experiment archive.
3. **Visual keywords:** hand-drawn sprite, red field, self-caricature, animation sketchbook, deliberately normal, playful technical confidence.
4. **First viewport:** A saturated red ground and hand-drawn black-and-white face lead. The rough line quality makes the portfolio feel authored before any project title is read.
5. **Typography:** Handwritten marks and sprite lettering define personality, supported by simple functional text. The register resembles an animated notebook rather than a polished agency system.
6. **Color:** Strong flat red, black and off-white form the recognizable core. Color blocking is direct and screen-graphic.
7. **Image or video:** Hand-drawn sprites, illustration and project clips replace generic mockups. Earlier work also reveals deep use of 3D experiments, shaders, sound and post-processing.
8. **3D and WebGL:** The current portfolio foregrounds stylized 2D sprites, while the lab demonstrates OGL, Three.js, custom shaders, FBM, cellular noise, modeling, sound and post-processing. The important lesson is choosing the medium that best expresses the current identity rather than always showing the most technical tool.
9. **Scroll and transitions:** The site behaves like a short animated strip. Sprite movement and sudden graphic changes create narrative rhythm.
10. **Mouse interaction:** Pointer and hover trigger hand-drawn reactions and project reveals, making the creator's character feel present.
11. **Most memorable element:** An experienced 3D developer choosing rough hand-drawn animation as the main signature.
12. **Three principles to learn:** Technical maturity permits aesthetic simplicity; a self-authored drawing can outperform expensive generic 3D; keep experiments separate from the main story when they would dilute the identity.
13. **Copy risks:** Red field, grinning self-caricature, hand-written `me` arrow and sprite-led portfolio would be direct imitation.
14. **Weaknesses to avoid:** Hand-drawn charm can become childish; red dominance can tire the eye; projects still need clear attribution and readable descriptions.
15. **Original transformation:** Site 02 can use direct manipulation and authored imperfection, but its visual language will be typographic, physical and cobalt-yellow rather than red cartoon sprites.

## Design Gene Library

### Transferable genes

| Gene | Evidence from references | Original use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular artifact | Alche's `A`, Sergey's floppy, Adrien's self-caricature | Give each route one ownable object that can be named from memory |
| Monumental and tiny scale contrast | Alche, Cappen, Joseph | Use scale contrast to establish hierarchy, then test small copy at 4K |
| Interface as worldview | bychudy, Joseph, Thingy & Thingy | Make navigation behavior express the brand, not just decorate it |
| Media-first confidence | Twice, Stiff | Let strong media own a viewport and reduce surrounding chrome |
| Motion as tone of voice | Stiff, Thingy & Thingy, Adrien | Define separate timing curves and physical rules for each route |
| Persistent spatial continuity | Alche, Joseph | Keep a scene evolving across chapters rather than resetting per section |
| Familiar mechanism, unfamiliar content | bychudy, Thingy & Thingy | Give experimental interactions understandable input and visible consequence |
| Typography as architecture | Cappen, Stiff, Sergey | Treat letterforms as compositional masses, not labels above cards |
| Authored imperfection | Adrien, Thingy & Thingy, Stiff | Use intentional material irregularity where it strengthens voice |
| Controlled restraint | Twice, Cappen | Reserve motion and color so visual peaks have contrast |

## Rules Carried Into Original Work

1. One signature interaction per route must have a clear input, transformation and consequence.
2. Only Site 01 and Site 04 may use cinematic full-scene Three.js as a core layer. Site 02 uses physical DOM/canvas behavior. Site 03 stays photography and type led.
3. No source palette is copied as a complete combination.
4. No recognizable source object, mascot, slogan, operating-system shell or project taxonomy is reused.
5. The four routes must not share hero structure, scroll logic or timing curves.
6. Every 3D scene needs narrative state changes, designed lighting, material response and a reduced-motion fallback.
7. Motion will be documented by purpose: hierarchy, storytelling, feedback or state transition.
8. High-resolution masters remain separate from compressed web delivery assets.
